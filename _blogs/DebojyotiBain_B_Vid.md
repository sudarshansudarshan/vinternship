When I started this journey as an intern at Vicharanashala (Lab for Education Design) , I was honestly just excited.

It felt like something new, something meaningful. I thought I’d just follow along, stay consistent, and things would work out.

That idea didn’t last very long.

When things didn’t go as expected
Very quickly, I realized this wasn’t going to be as smooth as I had imagined.

Showing up every day, sticking to fixed slots, keeping that 3.33% progress going on ViBe - it sounds simple, but doing it every single day is a different story.

Some days I didn’t feel like logging in. Some days I did, but nothing really made sense. And some days, even small things felt unnecessarily difficult.

It got frustrating at times.

Not because it was “too much”… but because I wasn’t used to being this consistent.

That confusing middle phase
There was this phase - hard to describe properly - where I wasn’t completely lost, but I wasn’t confident either.

Like, I’d understand things while watching… but the moment I tried doing them on my own, I’d get stuck.

And then the overthinking would start - am I even learning this the right way?

That kind of confusion is actually worse than not understanding anything at all.

What slowly changed
I didn’t have some big turning point where everything suddenly made sense.

It was more… gradual.

I just kept going.

Even on days when I wasn’t sure what I was doing. Even when progress felt slow. Even when I honestly felt like skipping.

And somewhere in between all that, something changed.

I stopped expecting things to feel easy. I stopped needing instant clarity.

I just focused on showing up.

And weirdly, that started working.

A different kind of viva
After finishing ViBe, I got nominated for the Phase 2 viva taken by Himanshu P Dev .

There were six of us together.

I expected it to be stressful, like a typical viva… but it didn’t feel like that at all.

It felt more like a group discussion or more like a team gossip.

We were listening to each other, adding points, questioning, thinking out loud. It wasn’t about giving perfect answers - it was more about learning and correcting errors leading to self improvement.

That was surprisingly meaningful for me.

And honestly, that’s where I ended up connecting with a few people I hadn’t known before - Chethana J R Vijayalaxmi Swami Harsha Vardhan Rao Ponnada Siddhi Satav Harshvardhan singh sikarwar 

We started discussing things, then working together on case studies… and we’re still doing that.

What stayed with me
I don’t remember every concept I learned.

But I clearly remember how this whole experience felt.

The excitement at the beginning. The frustration in the middle. The confusion that stayed for quite some time. And then slowly… getting a little more comfortable with all of it.

I think that’s what changed the most.

I don’t panic when things don’t make sense immediately anymore. I don’t wait for perfect explanations.

I just try to figure things out and keep moving.

Final thought
If I had to put it simply:

I didn’t become someone who find things easy, rather I became someone who doesn’t quit when things aren’t easy.

And I think that matters more.

Grateful to be part of this journey with Vicharanashala (Lab for Education Design) , and for all the guidance along the way - Sudarshan Iyengar, Nandan Prabhudesai, Jinal Gupta, Sakshi Sharma, Harshdeep R., Nitin Sankar, Meenakshi V, Rohit Sharma, Suruchi Mittal.
