My internship journey began with an email that read: "We are extending an internship opportunity, as you are enrolled in Prof. Sudarshan Iyengar's course." That mail felt like a blessing that came out of nowhere.

I was introduced to my course module — MERN. I assumed it would be one of those self-paced online courses you watch at your own leisure. But these modules were different: they enforced proctored learning. On the first day, I resisted it. I had to maintain the correct posture and answer quiz questions every single minute. I even tried switching to the YouTube lectures they provided as a supplement — and that's when something surprised me. I started missing the quizzes. The very thing I had resented on day one turned out to be the perfect driver of micro-learning. It gave meaning and value to every minute.

Then came another assumption I had to let go of. Since it was online, I figured I'd simply go through lectures, complete case studies, and eventually receive a certificate. But to my astonishment, Sudarshan Sir himself interacted with us directly. His mantra — consistency over hard work, or even smart work — is something I'll carry forward for life. Equally important were my mentors, who organized standup sessions and live sessions, and showed me what it truly means to be a mentor — through both their words and their actions. The activities pushed me well outside my comfort zone, but those were precisely the experiences I needed to grow.

Then came the third aspect: my peers. Being an introvert who takes years to make friends, I wondered how I could possibly connect with anyone in just two or three months. I was wrong again. In this internship, collaboration and teamwork are not phrases printed on a template — they are lived in their truest spirit. You genuinely cannot go far here without team spirit. As I write this, my heart fills with happiness. I've found my kind of people — those who share the same passion, the same curiosity, and the same desire to build things.

Although it's been only a month, I already feel like someone capable of writing production-level code. Our coursework and case studies ensure clarity of understanding and implementation — and knowing that my mentors and peers have my back makes all the difference. I always dreamed of what it would feel like to work in a lab, driven by purpose, creating things that matter. The VLEd Lab matched that dream. And beyond the learning, I have the rare opportunity to ask the creators themselves about the technology and its implementation. They don't just answer your questions — they encourage you to innovate.

A small moment captures this best. I have long been fascinated by large language models — the kind of curiosity that lives mostly in theory, in papers, in things you read late at night. What I didn't expect was the chance to actually contribute. Here, I got to work with my peers on enhancing Sakha, the Vinternship's own vLLM, through a query-feedback mechanism. It wasn't deeply technical work — but that almost made it more valuable. It sharpened my critical thinking in ways that pure implementation doesn't always force, and it lit something in me: a genuine curiosity about how these systems are actually built. I have studied the theory of artificial intelligence and machine learning in classrooms. But watching those theories quietly powering something real — something that responds, adapts, and learns — feels less like engineering and more like watching a kind of magic unfold.

I don't know what the end of this internship holds. But somewhere along the way, I have come to believe something I didn't quite believe before — that I can achieve anything I honestly put my heart and mind into. And for those moments when doubt creeps in, or when I falter, I know I won't be facing them alone. I have found mentors who will always treat me as one of their own.

*P.S. — To Dr. Sudarshan Iyengar: thank you for this initiative. Thank you for keeping the bar low enough that anyone could walk in — and raising it high enough that once you walk out, you stand on par with any capable engineer.*
