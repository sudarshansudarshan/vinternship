## Introduction

When I started my Vinternship, I had a simple expectation.

Learn MERN.  
Build projects.  
Get better at coding.

That’s it.

But after spending more than a month here, I started noticing something else.

The biggest change wasn’t in the code I was writing.

It was in the way I was thinking.

---

## The Journey

In the beginning, everything felt normal.

Learning through ViBe, going through concepts, trying things on my own — it all felt structured and manageable.

It was the usual flow:  
Understand → Apply → Move ahead

But things started changing when I moved to case studies.

They weren’t about just applying what I had learned.

They made me stop and think.

And honestly, that felt different.

---

## When Things Got Difficult (and Useful)

During the case studies, there were many times when:
- I didn’t fully understand the problem at first  
- My first approach didn’t work  
- I had to go back and rethink everything  

Sometimes, I spent more time thinking than coding.

At first, that felt frustrating.

But over time, I realized — that’s actually where the learning was happening.

I wasn’t just solving problems anymore.

I was trying to understand them.

---

## A Small but Important Shift

Earlier, I used to jump straight into coding.

Now, I pause a little.

Think about the problem.

Break it down.

Try to understand what’s actually being asked.

It’s not a big dramatic change.

But it’s there.

And it matters.

---

## Learning From People

Another part of this journey that I didn’t expect was how much I would learn from others.

Simple discussions with peers helped a lot.

Sometimes, just hearing someone else’s approach made things clearer.

It also made me realize that everyone is figuring things out in their own way.

That made the whole process feel less stressful.

---

## Standups and a Glimpse of the Real World

The standup sessions were something new for me.

At first, they felt like just updates.

But slowly, I started understanding their importance.

They show how communication and responsibility matter in real work.

It gave me a small idea of how things actually work in a professional environment.

---

## What I’m Taking From This

If I look back now, I didn’t just learn MERN.

I learned:
- to be more patient  
- to not rush towards answers  
- to stay with a problem even when it feels confusing  

And most importantly…

to think better.

---

## Final Reflection

This Vinternship didn’t just improve my coding.

It changed how I approach problems.

And somewhere in this journey…

I stopped trying to just *write code*.

And started learning how to **think like a developer.**
