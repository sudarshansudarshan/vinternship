My Learning Journey with ViBe: From Fear to Confidence

Nagashree Venkatesh

--

March 30, 2026

This internship was very different from the cliché internships we usually see.

It did not just teach me concepts—it taught me how to learn in the first place. The understanding it gave me was immense.

I truly appreciate the efforts of the team in conducting the standup sessions. The learning we got through those live sessions is something that cannot be expressed enough in words. The way case studies were given, along with a sample template, was a great way to build concepts in our minds while also giving us practical learning experience.

While going through the MERN learning, I never felt like I was just completing content. I was actually trying to understand what I was doing. Some parts were easy, some took more time, but I did not feel like stopping.

There were moments when I could not solve quizzes or case studies. I struggled. I felt stuck. But I learned to stand up on my own. It took patience—sometimes a lot of it—but it slowly trained me to be patient with my learning process.

This internship felt like the kind of learning I was always expecting—doing things on our own with minimal handholding. For me, that is very important. The guidance was always there, but in a way that allowed us to think and grow independently. Even the effort put into creating the learning videos is truly commendable.

Some days felt really heavy. But I still pushed myself to continue, because I had started enjoying the process of learning itself.

When I started this Vinternship, I had a closed mindset. I thought it would be like any other internship, and that thought itself felt frustrating. But slowly, things started to change.

The breakout room discussions, small moments of figuring things out, the feedback—even the mistakes—started to feel different. They were no longer signs of failure. They became part of the process.

I may still need more practice to gain expertise in the MERN stack, but now I feel confident that I have a strong foundation to build on.

What I understood from this experience is simple:

When learning is structured well, and when you are genuinely interested, effort does not feel forced.

You show up differently. 

You think differently. 

You grow without constantly questioning the process.

Instead of rushing to finish, I started focusing on understanding. Instead of seeing challenges as obstacles, I began to see them as part of the journey I chose.

I am generally a fearful person, and I started this journey with the same fear. But over time, that fear has slowly started turning into confidence.

And that, for me, is one of the biggest takeaways.
